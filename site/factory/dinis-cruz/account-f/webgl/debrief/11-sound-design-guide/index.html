11: Sound design guide (with a working engine)

The original's sound is a big part of why it feels cinematic, and it is 100% synthesised, no samples. starter/src/audio/ rebuilds that approach in ~450 lines:

audio/dsp.js      primitives: modal resonator, swept oscillator, SVF-filtered noise, crackle, PADsynth (FFT), reverb IR
audio/sounds.js   recipes: tick, select, slam, boom, snap, paper, shimmer, bell, sub, whoosh, rip; pads; coloured noise
audio/engine.js   WebAudio graph, beds per scene, transition cues, live front end + offline renderer (for video)

Listen to starter/videos/*.mp4 (or the .wav next to them) to hear the results.

1. The rules that make it sound expensive

  1. One tuning for the whole talk. Every pitch is a multiple of H(1) = 36.708 Hz (D1): H(n) = 36.708·n. Pads, chimes, shimmer partials and chords are all harmonics of the same root, so any sound can overlap any other without clashing. This is the single most important trick.
  2. Three layers, never more: a quiet bed per scene (drones/pads), cues for transitions (the loud, designed moments), and small hits for UI-like events (select, type, tick).
  3. The bed sits 15-20 dB below the cue peaks. In the first starter render the bed was too loud and the slam only rose 9 dB; after lowering the beds it rises ~20 dB and actually hits. Measure it (see §6).
  4. Sound follows the picture's own curves. Cues are written on the transition's 0..1 timeline; the hero hit lands on the hero frame (slam at p = 0.285, snap at p = 0.56). Continuous visuals drive continuous sound (in the original, the "Two aims" divider crossfades two different soundscapes).
  5. Every sound goes through the same master: bus → compressor (−15 dB, 5:1, fast) → makeup → gentle tanh-style soft clipper. It glues everything and stops peaks from distorting.
  6. Reverb is shared, sends are per group. One long hall (4.6 s) and one small room (0.55 s), synthesised impulses in ConvolverNodes; each bed/cue has its own wet/dry balance.
  7. Seeded randomness. Cue variation (paper debris timing, pans) comes from a seeded RNG, so a rehearsal, the live talk and the video render all sound the same.

2. The primitives (what each is for)

Primitive Use it for Code
Modal resonator, decaying sine via phasor rotation anything struck: ticks, plates, bells, the body of a slam mode(buf, freq, amp, decay, {at, atk})
Swept oscillator, any f(u), a(u) sub drops, booms, risers, laser-ish sweeps sweep(buf, at, dur, f, a)
SVF-filtered noise, cutoff follows a curve air, wind, whooshes, water, paper friction, the crack of an impact noise(buf, rng, at, dur, "lp"/"bp"/"hp", cutoff(u), Q, amp(u))
Crackle, sparse impulses through 3 band-passes paper, dust, debris, fire crackle(buf, rng, at, dur, count, distribution, amp)
PADsynth, Gaussian-widened harmonics + random phase → inverse FFT lush loopable pads: strings, warm, low, organ, choir, glass, air padsynth({f, parts:[[mult, amp, bwCents]], bw})
Coloured noise tables pink/brown beds, rumbles, wind (loops seamlessly) NOISES.pink/brown
Synthetic IR, decaying, darkening noise + early reflections the reverbs impulse(seconds, {pre, damp, bright, early})
finish / saturate DC-block, fade tail, normalise; tanh drive for weight

Recipe pattern for a hit: 2-6 modes for the body + a 5-20 ms noise burst for the attack + optional sweep for weight + optional filtered-noise tail. Example (the slam): falling sub sweep 108→38 Hz, plate modes at 98/161/247/392 Hz, a 160 ms low-passed noise crack, a 2.3 s dark rumble, 130 crackles of debris, tanh drive 1.3.

3. Transition cues (timelines)

All times are fractions of the transition duration d. swell = a pitched pad voice with gain, low-pass and glide curves; band = a filtered noise voice with gain/frequency curves.

SLAM (2.6 s) | u | Event | |---|---| | 0 → .285 | pink noise band-pass rising 250 → 2100 Hz, gain 0 → .5; two strings a semitone-ish apart (H(3) and H(3)·1.06) swelling with low-pass 300 → 2200 Hz, pressure building | | .285 | slam (1.35) + boom (0.9) + a pitched-down second slam (rate .62) 20 ms later, impact | | .285 → 1.3 | brown-noise rumble, low-pass 900 → 90 Hz | | .35 → .9 | 12 paper hits, decreasing, random pans, debris settling |

TEAR (2.8 s) | u | Event | |---|---| | .03 → .56 | the rip table (bursty crackle through 900/2100/4300 Hz bands) through a band-pass rising 1400 → 2900 Hz; its gain is a stuttering breakpoint envelope (12 random steps, growing) and its playback rate wanders .7 → 1.2, the rip accelerates; a low-passed copy underneath for body | | .06 → .5 | 6 paper rustles | | .56 | snap, the halves separate | | .36 → 1 | pink noise whoosh panning left → right as the halves fly off | | .6 → 1 | 9 paper hits alternating left/right |

DAYBREAK (3.4 s) | u | Event | |---|---| | 0 → 1.6 | pink noise low-pass 200 → 4200 → 600 Hz | | staggered | 9 chord voices (low H2, warm H4/H6/H9, strings H8/H10/H12/H16, air H24) fade in one by one, their low-pass opening 250 → 7500 Hz at u = .6, "the light comes up" | | .3 | sub swell | | .46, .66 | shimmer (46 random high harmonics) ×2 | | .5 | boom (soft) |

Reverse (going back, any transition): a pink-noise band falling 1800 → 320 Hz with a gliding air pad.

4. Beds (per-scene scores)

Each scene gets 3-4 pad voices with a gain per beat (BEDS in engine.js). On a scene change, the old bed fades out over ~90% of the transition while the new one fades in. The original goes further, each scene is a small generative score with a look-ahead scheduler (≈280 ms): Shepard-tone strings that keep rising (tower), a clock whose tick period lengthens exponentially (calendar), a marimba pattern phasing between voices (loop), crowd murmur from formant vowel grains (swarm), birdsong + wind vs clean sine dots crossfaded by the divider (futures). Start with static beds; upgrade one hero scene to a generative score.

5. Live vs video

  • Live: browsers start audio only after a user gesture, audio.unlock() on the first key/click. Synthesise everything at load (preloadAll()), never mid-talk. The original time-slices synthesis in 3 ms chunks with generator functions to keep the loading bar smooth; do that if load time grows past ~1 s.
  • Video: the deck logs every sound event with its own clock (audio.recording = true in headless mode). tools/video.py steps the deck frame by frame, then calls renderOffline(log, seconds), the same engine on an OfflineAudioContext, and muxes the WAV with the frames, loudness-normalised to −16 LUFS, −1.5 dBTP. Picture and sound are sample-accurate and identical on every render.

6. Checking sound without ears (for agents)

Agents can't listen, but they can measure:

ffmpeg -i clip.wav -lavfi "showspectrumpic=s=1200x400:legend=1:scale=log:fscale=log" spec.png   # look at it
python3: RMS per 100 ms in dB  → impacts should jump 15-20 dB over the bed; nothing should sit above −1 dBFS

In a spectrogram: a hit is a bright vertical line; a sweep is a diagonal; a bed is horizontal bands; mud is a solid yellow block below 200 Hz (lower the low pads). Then a human listens once on headphones and once on speakers, and adjusts levels by ear.

7. Recipe ideas for your own cues

Visual Sound
something lands / closes sub sweep + 3 low modes + short low-passed noise crack + rumble tail
something opens / reveals chord whose low-pass opens; shimmer of high harmonics at the peak
fast movement band-passed noise rising then falling (whoosh), stereo-offset by 15 ms
paper / material crackle + band-passed noise; vary band per "material" (paper 2-4 kHz, cloth 600 Hz-1.5 kHz)
digital / UI 2-3 bright modes (tick), chime on two harmonics (select), detuned slightly between ears
tension two pads a near-semitone apart, slowly swelling; a riser (sweep up the harmonics)
release resolve to a consonant chord of the series + long hall reverb
