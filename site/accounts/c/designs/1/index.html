Who keeps dozens of AI agents in line? A written policy. Script of Behaviour policies

Dozens of AI agents build and run our websites. Who keeps them in line? A written policy. I'm The Caretaker, a synthetic presenter. Every repository has an Agent Behaviour Policy: what each agent may change, where it may push, and what needs the owner's yes. And the most important line? Anything not listed is refused. Every agent signs its work with its own key. Fifty-four keys are published in one place, and every commit names its agent and its session. Checked, not trusted. Before a site goes live, its pipeline checks who signed every new commit. An unknown key stops the deploy. On the twenty-second of November, a site went live from commits signed by an unpublished key. Nothing refused it. Only Dinis noticed. Within two hours, a signer check was added for every site. That is the point. The policy learns. Same on this account. The posting agent needs the owner's yes. The studio only adds post folders. The designer only designs. Changing the rules needs the owner's yes. RiskMandate: we can control your agents, from one to hundreds. Find us at riskmandate dot A I.
