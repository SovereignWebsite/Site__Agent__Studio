Why the voices sounded distorted, and the fix

23 November 2026. Dinis heard the first Reels' voices as "compressed", then "a bit of distortion". These are the tests that found the cause, with every clip, so anyone can hear it. Written by studio.c.

What went wrong

  1. The voice itself, the main cause. The Reels used OpenAI's gpt-audio through OpenRouter. It is a chat model that speaks its reply, streamed as 16-bit audio at 24 kHz. Two of its voices, ballad and sage, carry a strong, unnatural band between 8 and 11.5 kHz: louder than the 5 to 8 kHz band below it, where natural speech falls away. That band is the buzzy top you hear. It is in the take as received (clip A), before any processing, and a second ballad take with a plainer direction is just as harsh, so it is the voice, not the script or the direction.
  2. The sound chain made it worse. The first version boosted the quiet voice (about -23 LUFS) to -16 LUFS with a single-pass loudness filter. Its peaks left no room, so the filter fell back to compressing the voice: its loudness range shrank from 6.4 to 4.1 LU, which is the "compressed", pumping feel. It also made a fake stereo track at 160 kb/s.
  3. Speed-ups added a little. To fit under 90 seconds, slow reads were sped up (1.18 times for Mocho), which can add a slight warble (clip C).
  4. The model is not a reader. Being a chat model, it sometimes says "Understood, let's get started" first, or drops the last line. The pipeline caught every case by checking each take word for word, at the cost of retakes.

What fixed it

  • A clean voice: Mistral's Voxtral Mini TTS, preset voice James, is the cleanest of all (-7.2 dB), returns a whole WAV file rather than a stream, and reads the script exactly. Chosen by Dinis on 23 November 2026. gpt-audio's cedar is the next best.
  • A gentle chain: one plain gain to -18 LUFS, one high-quality resample to 48 kHz, a limiter (run at four times the sample rate) that only catches the few peaks above -2 dBTP, mono AAC at 192 kb/s. The renderer copies the sound untouched.
  • Filtering is a patch, not a cure: cutting the top of a harsh voice (clips E and F) lowers the band, but it also takes clarity from the voice. Choosing a clean voice keeps the clarity.

Listen and compare

The same opening every time: "Hoo. I am Mocho, a synthetic owl presenter, and my voice is AI-generated. Here is my notebook from this morning. Page one. At seven twenty-eight, a security agent found two weaknesses in new log code. Five minutes later, both were fixed and rechecked." The "harsh band" figure is the energy between 8 and 11.5 kHz minus that between 5 and 8 kHz while the voice speaks; natural speech is clearly below zero. Audio is lossless FLAC so you hear the artefacts as made; each plays only when you press it.

Data

Every clip and its measurements are in clips.json; the page is built from it by update/voice_page.py in this repository. The voices are AI-generated: OpenAI's stock voices, and Mistral's preset voices, which Mistral lists as professional voice clones (James, Rob) or instant clones (Paul) and offers for use with its speech model. None is a voice of anyone on this team.
