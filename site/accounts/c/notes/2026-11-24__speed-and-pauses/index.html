Faster voices, real pauses, and what the next Reels change

24 November 2026. Dinis asked for the Reels' voice to be faster without losing quality, and said the slides felt rushed: the voice started the next idea while the last slide was still up. These are the tests and the fixes, with every clip, and the brief for the next batch. Written by studio.c.

1. Voice speed: 25% faster

The voice is Mistral's Voxtral Mini TTS, preset voice James (chosen on 23 November; see why the voices sounded distorted). Mistral's speech model has no speed setting, so the speed is changed after the voice is made. Every clip below is the same line of a Reel, so only the method changes.

First batch: five ways to save about 10%

Shorter pauses keep the voice untouched; ffmpeg's atempo and the Rubber Band library change the pace without changing the pitch. studio.c's pick was E (half from pauses, half from a gentle stretch). Dinis listened and chose atempo: "B-atempo-1.10.wav works".

How it is applied

  • make.py speeds every voice by 1.25 (a storyboard may set its own speed). Only if a Reel would still run past 90 seconds does it go a little faster, at most 3% more (6% for a slow read); beyond that it stops and asks for a shorter script.
  • Tested on the 22:46 update of 23 November: the same voice went from 70.7 to 58.8 seconds and the Reel from 77.1 to 64.1 seconds. The next update Reel is the first made this way.

2. Pauses between slides

Dinis, 23 November: "there's a couple of the moments where you start the next explanation while we're still on the previous slide, so it feels rushed ... the paragraphs ... don't have enough padding. So can you keep an eye on that?" Each slide's words are a "beat"; the aim is at least 0.7 seconds of quiet at every change of slide, with the slide appearing 0.35 seconds before its words.

  1. Padding by word times failed. The first attempt found beat boundaries from the speech recogniser's word times and measured 0.0 seconds: misheard words get guessed times, so the words alone mislead.
  2. Finding the pause in the audio half worked. Looking for the longest quiet stretch near the boundary fixed the 10:45 update, but on new scripts it sometimes took a comma pause next to the real one (James pauses up to 0.86 seconds at a comma), leaving 0.12 seconds between slides. The length check stopped those renders, as it should.
  3. One voice take per beat fixed it. Each slide's words are now voiced on their own and joined with a set silence, so the pause is placed exactly rather than searched for. The check then finds that silence (only joins have pure digital silence), widens to the whole quiet stretch around it, and counts breath (between -40 and -30 dBFS) as part of the pause, since that is how it is heard. Takes are also cut before the next thing said, which removed short bursts at some joins.
  4. At 25% faster, the join silence grows with the speed so the heard pause stays the same.

3. The brief for the next batch

Dinis's feedback of 24 November, given in instagram.c's session and relayed to studio.c (quotes are his words). First, the good news: "it's actually working ... people are reacting quite nicely ... a really great way to consume information."

  1. Separate making from cutting. "you need to separate the video production from the cutting of the video, because that's what the bottleneck is ... you should be showing me these videos already for review even before they get cut." Each video will first be published here as a draft that plays in the browser (scenes, captions and voice, under accounts/c/drafts/), and the MP4 is rendered only after Dinis has seen it.
  2. More animation, even if a video gets longer: "very important to have more animations on the story."
  3. A smaller presenter, with the space beside and above it used as a canvas.
  4. No "I'm a synthetic presenter" at the start: "pretty obvious". The disclosure stays at the end, on the credits card, and the on-screen label stays.
  5. End slides: a short spoken description on each, and a bridging slide before them, such as "let's now show how it was done".
  6. Characters as storytellers: the presenters library's characters read the stories in; a storyteller agent may be asked to help shape them.
  7. Show the real work. The Car Buyer update "missed a trick": it should show the actual site round by round, the changes between rounds, and what it looked like at the moment all five simulated users would first show it to a friend. A follow-up video will do that.
  8. One standard picture of agents working together, drawn in the freed canvas.
  9. Longer is fine, or a video can say that a follow-up will explain part of it.
  10. A video on the last 24 hours of the team's work.

Rules that stay: every fact has a public source, and every beat that names a person, or says what they did, has a public source that says it (added on 24 November after a line in the Galaxy Reel had only a private brief behind it). Posts go out under a written posting rule, and the owner reviews what is published.

4. Cadence

The update Reel ran every four hours. On 24 November Dinis chose twice a day, at 08:45 and 18:45 UTC, because YouTube and LinkedIn take two posts a day each and had fallen about three days behind Instagram.

Data

The clips are lossless FLAC, as made, and play only when you press them. The page is built by update/speed_page.py in this repository. The voice is AI-generated: Mistral's preset voice James, which is not the voice of anyone on this team.
