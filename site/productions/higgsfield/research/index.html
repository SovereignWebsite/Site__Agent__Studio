Higgsfield: may we offer it to our users, and how?

Dinis Cruz asked (through c.maestro, 28 November 2026): may images made with Higgsfield be used and resold; could we offer a free or paid “try an infographic” service to our users; does Higgsfield have an API; can a browser hold a small key; do we need a proxy? This page answers from Higgsfield's own published pages only, each read on 28 November 2026. It is not legal advice: the places where counsel is needed are marked.

The short answer

  • Ownership: you own what you generate and may use it commercially, on any plan.
  • Offering generation to our users: allowed through the API, but only inside an app that adds real function of its own, with our own terms for those users, and never by passing the API or its keys straight through. Plain resale of access needs Higgsfield's written consent.
  • API: yes, at api.higgsfield.ai, pay per image from a prepaid balance (minimum top-up $5), separate from the app subscription.
  • Browser keys: no. The docs forbid keys in browser code and describe no scoped, short-lived or per-user keys. A small proxy is required.
  • First step: an invite-only proxy on a $5 prepaid balance with automatic top-up off, one cheap image model, and the words and numbers of the infographic drawn by our own code. Counsel reads the API Terms before anything is public.

1. Who owns the outputs, and may we offer generation to others?

  • “Company does not claim ownership of any of your Inputs or Outputs, nor does it restrict your commercial use of Outputs.” Terms of Use, section 4.4. Terms of Use (last updated 23 October 2026).
  • “The Terms of Use do not tie commercial use rights to a specific plan.” Paid plans differ in watermark-free downloads, not in rights. Help: who owns my generations.
  • Rights in exported outputs survive cancellation (Terms of Use 4.4). Outputs are not exclusive: “Other users may receive an Output that is similar or possibly identical to yours”, and inputs and outputs may be used to train Higgsfield's models (Terms of Use 4.4).
  • The app licence covers personal or internal business use “or, where applicable, to build and operate applications for your end users in accordance with Section 11” (Terms of Use 1.2). Section 11 (developer terms) was not read verbatim; a summary of it says it bars sublicensing and resale. Unconfirmed.
  • The app itself may not be resold or hosted for others: no one may “license, resell, rent, transfer, assign, reproduce, distribute, host, or otherwise commercially exploit the Service” (Terms of Use 5.2(i)); accounts and logins may not be shared (2.4).
  • The API is the route for a service to our users, under its own terms (API Terms of Service, last updated 2 September 2026):
    • “You shall not expose any of the API Service (including API endpoints or API Keys) directly to any End User” (4.1). End users may use it only through an app that “adds material functionality beyond merely providing access to the API Service” (4.1).
    • No resale, lease, sublicence or distribution of the API on a standalone basis or as a competing generation service, and no service-bureau use, “except with Company's prior written consent” (4.2).
    • We must have “legally enforceable agreements with each of your End Users”, at least as protective of Higgsfield (4.4), and we are “responsible and liable for the acts and omissions of your End Users” (4.5).
  • Free or paid: the pages read make no distinction. A free trial inside our own app looks the safer reading; charging for generation that is little more than access looks closest to 4.2. Counsel needed on both, and on whether an infographic tool counts as “material functionality”.
  • AI disclosure: “Where required by applicable law, you will disclose that Output is artificially generated or manipulated” (Terms of Use 5.5), and we must require our end users to do the same (API Terms 12.3). Higgsfield may embed watermarks or provenance data such as C2PA, which must not be removed (Terms of Use 6.4, 5.5). Our own rule goes further: “made by AI” on every frame we show.
  • Real people: no deceptive deepfakes or impersonation (API Terms 6.1); documented consent for any real person simulated (6.2); face swap, lip sync, avatar and Soul ID endpoints are “Protected Features” that may need verification (1.5(d), 2.2). A user-facing tool should block these entirely.

2. The API

  • Base address: https://api.higgsfield.ai. API FAQ.
  • Authentication: a key ID and a secret, sent as Authorization: Key KEY_ID:KEY_SECRET. API authentication. Keys belong to a workspace, not a person (API Terms 2.1); test keys run in dry-run mode only (9.6).
  • Limits: “Creating a key unlocks 20 concurrent requests” (Help: what is the Higgsfield API); “Rate limits depend on your account and the selected model” (FAQ). Requests over the limit are queued or rejected with a retryable error (API Terms 3.2).
  • Billing: a prepaid API balance “separate from any consumer Subscription or Credits”, and a consumer, Team or Scale subscription “does not include API access” (API Terms 9.1, 1.2). Minimum top-up $5; funds expire after a year; failed requests are not billed (help page; API Terms 9.2, 9.4). At zero balance new requests are rejected (API Terms 9.3; the help page says they wait).

3. Keys in the browser, or a proxy?

  • “Do not call the API directly from browser or mobile application code.” (authentication page); “Do not use a Bearer token or expose the secret in browser or mobile code.” (FAQ); “you shall not embed API Keys in client-side or publicly accessible code” (API Terms 2.1).
  • None of the pages read describes scoped, short-lived, per-user keys or per-key spend limits. Not found; ask Higgsfield before assuming they do not exist.

So: a small proxy. Our websites are static files with no server code, so the proxy lives outside them, as one small serverless function:

  1. Holds the key in the platform's secret store; it never reaches a browser or a repository.
  2. Knows who asks: each request is signed by the user's own browser key (the site's sign-in already makes one per browser) or carries a short-lived token the proxy issued; unsigned requests are refused.
  3. Per-user quota: for example 5 images a day per user; a global daily cap below the balance.
  4. Spend cap that cannot be overrun: the prepaid balance itself, with automatic top-up off; at zero, requests are rejected.
  5. Allow-list: one or two image models only; no video, no uploads of faces, none of the Protected Features.
  6. Abuse limits: prompt length cap, a block list for real names and likeness requests, a per-address rate limit, and a log of every request with its cost (our ledger).
  7. Adds material function: the proxy asks for an illustration only; our own code sets the infographic's words and numbers on top (image models still garble text, and our rule keeps words out of generated pictures). That is both better infographics and the “material functionality” API Terms 4.1 asks for.
  8. Labels the result: “Image made by AI” inside the picture; any C2PA data left intact.

Where it runs: any small serverless platform with a secret store (one function, one small table for quotas). Which one, and its cost, is a choice for whoever keeps the team's infrastructure; not priced here.

4. Recommendation

  1. Now, cheapest and safe: open an API balance with the $5 minimum, automatic top-up off. At Soul 2's listed price that is about 1,500 images; at Recraft 4.1's, about 140. Build the proxy above for signed-in, invited users only, one model, 5 images a day each.
  2. Use it ourselves first: the media desk's agents can use the same API from server-side code, which may be cheaper and faster for them than a subscription and relays. This feeds the renewal trial: the API is a separate product, pay per use, and could replace or shrink the subscription.
  3. Before any public free offer: counsel reads API Terms 4.1, 4.2, 4.4 and 12.3 and Terms of Use section 11, and we write short terms for our users that pass Higgsfield's down.
  4. Before charging: ask Higgsfield in writing; a paid offer is the closest to the resale clause (4.2).
