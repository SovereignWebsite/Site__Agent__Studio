# The critic's rubric

What the critic checks, on every script and every scene, before a person is asked to look. It comes from outside feedback
on an earlier Reel that Dinis Cruz shared on 27 November 2026 (he agreed with it), and from his own note on these productions:
"you got the right story, you just don't have the good way to tell the story ... this just looks at a more polished version of PowerPoint".

1. **So what?** After every beat, ask "so what?". If the script does not answer it, the beat is a fact, not a story. Cut it or connect it.
2. **One story.** One through-line a viewer can say back in a sentence. Facts that do not serve it go, however true.
3. **The best line first.** The strongest line lands in the first five seconds, not at 1:12. A Reel cut runs 60 seconds or less.
4. **A picture of the idea.** Each scene must still say something with its words removed. A title and a list over moving dots is a slide.
5. **One visual language.** One palette, one type pairing, a few motifs that return and change. Variety inside it, not a new style each scene.
6. **Motion that means something.** Movement shows cause, growth, loss or contrast. No movement only for decoration, no dead time.
7. **Sound in one piece.** One bed, gentle effects under it, the voice on top. Effects never fight the music.
8. **True and labelled.** Every beat has a public source. The film says its voice is made by AI: in every frame, or, where the owner has chosen it (Dinis Cruz, 28 November 2026: "I think you just need to have that disclaimer at the end"), on the closing scene only. No real person's likeness or voice.

The critic scores each point 0 to 2 with one sentence of evidence, names the single change that would help most, and never rewrites the work itself.
